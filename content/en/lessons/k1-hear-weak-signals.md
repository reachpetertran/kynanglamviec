## 1. Objectives

- **Respond to bad news in 3 steps:** thank, ask for facts, ask what is needed.
- **Set up a bad-news channel:** which news must reach the leadership team within 48 hours, through whom, and who may skip a level.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

2. Long waits; several deciders; pushed upwards.

## 3. Explanation

The higher you go, the less bad news you hear. Not because less goes wrong. Each level filters news, out of fear of blame or a wish to fix it first.

**Weak signals** are small, early, unclear signs. For example: a major client is slow to reply to emails, or two strong people in one department resign together. Ignore weak signals, and you will be handling a crisis later.

**Psychological safety.** Edmondson (1999) studied 51 teams in a manufacturing company. Teams with high psychological safety asked questions, reported errors and sought feedback more. These learning behaviours were linked to better team performance. Psychological safety means believing that speaking up will not lead to punishment or embarrassment.

At organisation level, how leaders react to bad news decides whether anyone reports it next time.

<div class="diagram"><p class="dg-title">Three steps when you hear bad news</p><ol class="steps">
<li><b>Thank the person</b><span>Before any question. They have just done something hard.</span></li>
<li><b>Ask for facts</b><span>What happened, when, who is affected. Not yet whose fault.</span></li>
<li><b>Ask what is needed</b><span>What do you need me to decide or support?</span></li>
</ol></div>

Finding causes and responsibility still matters. But do it later, through a proper process, not in the first minute.

**The bad-news channel.** Write down three things. One: which news must reach the leadership team within 48 hours, such as safety, major clients, legal and finance. Two: who it passes through. Three: when someone may skip a level, for example when they fear retaliation.

**Go and listen yourself.** Regularly meet front-line staff without their direct manager present. Ask: "What makes your work harder than it needs to be?" Write it down, and report back on what you did with it.

## 4. Workplace example

> **Hypothetical scenario.** A construction company runs 12 sites. The CEO, Maria, notices that weekly reports always say "safe, on schedule".

She visits a site unannounced. A foreman tells her: scaffolding on floor 9 was reported damaged 3 weeks ago, and nobody has fixed it. He was reluctant to report it upwards, because last time he was blamed for "letting it happen".

She thanks him first. She asks what happened, since when, and who is working there. Then she asks what he needs. He needs work on floor 9 stopped and replacement materials. She decides on the spot.

The following week, she sets up Sheet 6. **News that must arrive within 48 hours:** safety incidents, including near misses; delays of more than 2 weeks; disputes with the client. **Through:** site manager to construction director. **Skip a level:** straight to the head of safety if not dealt with in 24 hours.

She also tells site managers clearly: reporting early will not be blamed; hiding news will be looked into.

**Why this works:** her first reaction makes the reporter feel safe. The channel is clear, so news does not depend on each person's courage.

## 5. Practice

> **Hypothetical scenario.** You are retail director of an electronics chain. A store manager calls: "I'm sorry, yesterday the store sold an item at the wrong price, and we've lost a significant amount."

Write the 3 sentences you would say, following the 3 steps. List 3 types of news that should be in the retail division's bad-news channel.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Your first sentence should not be "How could you let this happen?"
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Think of news that would cost the whole chain if heard late: money, customers, safety, legal.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Model answer</summary>

1. "Thank you for telling me straight away."
2. "Which item was mispriced, and since when? How many orders were sold?"
3. "What do you need from me to stop it and handle it with customers?"

**Bad-news channel:** pricing errors or losses above an amount the chain sets; customer complaints at risk of going viral on social media; electrical safety incidents or fire.

(The details are hypothetical.)
</details>
