## 1. Objectives

- Describe a problem with: **what happened, when, the impact, what you have tried**.
- Bring **two options** and a recommendation before asking your manager.

## 3. Explanation

When something goes wrong, new people often ask straight away: "The machine's broken, what do I do?" The other person has to ask many questions to understand. A better way has two steps.

**Step 1. Describe the problem in 4 points.**

| Point | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What happened | What exactly do you see? | The printer shows "paper jam" but no paper is jammed |
| When | Since when? Does it repeat? | Since 9:00 this morning, 5 times |
| Impact | Which work, which people are affected? | 30 invoices not yet printed |
| Tried | What have you done? | Switched it off and on, checked the tray |

A good description helps others understand at once. Sometimes, while writing it, you spot the cause yourself.

**Step 2. Bring two options.** Before asking, think of at least two ways to handle it. What are the pros and cons of each? Then say which one you recommend.

<div class="flow" markdown="1">
<span>Describe 4 points</span>
<span>Two options</span>
<span>Recommendation</span>
<span>Ask for a decision</span>
</div>

The benefit: your manager decides faster. You also practise solving problems yourself.

**Important notes:**

- For safety incidents, such as fire, sparks or someone hurt, report at once. Do not wait to think of options.
- Options must be within your authority. Leave anything beyond it to the person who decides.
- If you are not sure, say so. Do not guess a cause and present it as fact.

## 4. Workplace example

> **Hypothetical scenario.** Laura is a receptionist at a private dental clinic. Since yesterday, the booking software has not sent appointment reminders to patients.

Laura messages her manager:

> "Hi Grace, I'm reporting a problem with the booking software.
>
> **What happened:** reminder texts are not sending; the screen says 'send failed'. **When:** since 14:00 yesterday. **Impact:** 12 patients booked for tomorrow have not had a reminder. **Tried:** logged out and back in; still failing.
>
> **Options:** (1) I phone the 12 patients, about 40 minutes. (2) I report it to the software support team and wait for a fix.
>
> I suggest doing both: call the patients now and report it to support. If you agree, I'll start right away."

**Why this works:** The manager understands the problem at once. Laura has a concrete recommendation, so the manager only needs to agree.

## 5. Practice

> **Hypothetical scenario.** You are a cashier at a small supermarket. Since the start of your shift, the card machine has declined 3 payments in a row. Cash payments work as usual.

Write a message to your shift leader: the 4 points, two options and a recommendation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

What happened: what does the machine say? When: since what time? Impact: customers cannot pay by card.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Possible options: suggest customers pay by bank transfer or cash; call the bank's helpline; use the machine at another till if there is one.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Model answer</summary>

"Hi Paul, the card machine at till 2 isn't working. **What happened:** it declined 3 payments with 'connection failed'. **When:** since 8:15. **Impact:** card customers have to pay another way. **Tried:** switched it off and on.

**Options:** (1) I suggest bank transfer or cash and put up a notice. (2) I call the bank's helpline to report it.

I suggest I do (1) now, and you do (2), since you have the merchant code. Is that OK?"

(Names and details are hypothetical.)
</details>

## 7. Reflection

1. The last time you asked your manager about a problem, did you describe all 4 points?
2. Was there a situation you could have solved yourself if you had thought of two options first?

## 8. Real-world application

In the next 7 days, choose the first problem you need to ask someone about. Write the 4 points and two options before you ask. Note the result: how the other person reacted, and how long it took to solve.
