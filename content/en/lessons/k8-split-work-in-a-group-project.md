## 1. Objectives

- Set up a **task board** with: part of the work, owner, deadline, output.
- Set **one shared check-in** before the final deadline.

## 3. Explanation

In group projects at school or at a part-time job, a common problem is: nobody is sure who does what. So some parts get done twice, and some not at all. Near the deadline, one or two people end up carrying everything.

**The tool: a task board.** At the first meeting, the group fills in this board:

| Part of the work | Owner | Deadline | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survey 30 customers | Max | Wednesday | Survey results file |
| Write the analysis | Ana | Friday | 2-page document |
| Make the slides | Iris | Saturday | 10 slides |

Three principles:

1. **Each part has only one owner.** Several people can work on it. But only one person is responsible in the end.
2. **The output must be visible.** "Survey results file" is clearer than "handle the survey".
3. **Part deadlines come before the final deadline.** You need time to combine and fix.

**A shared check-in.** Choose a session partway through, a little after halfway. Everyone shows their draft. The aim is to spot slow or off-track parts early.

<div class="flow" markdown="1">
<span>First meeting: board</span>
<span>Work</span>
<span>Check-in</span>
<span>Combine and fix</span>
<span>Submit</span>
</div>

**When someone is behind.** Ask early and specifically: "How far along is the survey, Max? Do you need any help?" Avoid criticising in front of the group. Find a way to unblock the work first.

Keep the board where everyone can see it, such as the group chat or a shared spreadsheet.

## 4. Workplace example

> **Hypothetical scenario.** Four second-year students are doing a marketing project: a promotion plan for a sandwich shop near campus. It is due next Monday.

At the first meeting, the group leader, Zoe, suggests setting up the board right away. The group agrees:

- Survey 30 customers – Kai – done Wednesday – results file.
- Competitor analysis of 3 shops – Nina – done Thursday – 1-page summary.
- Write the proposal – Zoe – done Friday – 3 pages.
- Slides and presentation practice – Sam – done Sunday – 12 slides.

Check-in: Thursday evening, a 30-minute online meeting where everyone shows their draft.

On Thursday evening, Kai has surveyed only 15 customers. The group agrees that Nina will help with the survey, since her part is done.

**Why this works:** each part has one owner and one output. The check-in spots the slow part while there are still three days left.

## 5. Practice

> **Hypothetical scenario.** You and 2 other part-timers at a café are asked to decorate the café for the winter holidays. The manager needs it done by the evening of the 22nd. The work: buy decorations, put up the tree, apply window stickers, take photos for the café's page.

Make a 4-column task board and choose one check-in.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Which task must come first? Buying the decorations must be done before the tree and the stickers.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

The check-in should come after shopping, before decorating starts.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Model answer</summary>

| Part of the work | Owner | Deadline | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy decorations from the list | You | 18th | All items, with receipts |
| Put up the tree | Mia | 20th | Finished tree in the corner |
| Apply window stickers | Jon | 20th | 2 windows done |
| Take photos, send to manager to approve | Mia | 21st | 5 approved photos |

Check-in: afternoon of the 18th; all three check the purchases against the list.

(Names and dates are hypothetical.)
</details>
