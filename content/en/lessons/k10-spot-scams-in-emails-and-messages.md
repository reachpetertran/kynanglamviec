## 1. Objectives

- **Point out at least 2 warning signs** of a scam in a work email or message.
- **Handle it in 3 steps:** stop, check through another channel, tell the right person.

## 2. Retrieval warm-up

1. What are the three habits for protecting your accounts?
2. Why is anyone who asks for your one-time code almost certainly a scammer?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Long passwords, a different one for each place; turn on two-step verification; never give your password or one-time code to anyone.
2. Because the one-time code is the last key for getting into an account or moving money.

Review: [Protect your passwords and personal information](../k10-passwords-and-personal-information/).
</details>

## 3. Explanation

Scammers usually do not break into computers. They trick **people** into opening the door themselves: clicking a link, sending a password, transferring money. New employees are easy targets, because they do not know the processes yet and feel shy about asking.

**Five common warning signs:**

<div class="diagram"><p class="dg-title">Five warning signs of a scam message</p><ol class="steps">
<li><b>Urgency</b><span>"Within 30 minutes", "your account will be locked", "the boss needs it now".</span></li>
<li><b>Asking for secrets</b><span>Passwords, one-time codes, photos of your ID or bank card.</span></li>
<li><b>Asking for a transfer or new bank details</b><span>Especially when it skips the normal approval process.</span></li>
<li><b>An address that looks almost right</b><span>A domain with an extra letter, a different ending, or one character changed.</span></li>
<li><b>Pretending to be someone you know</b><span>A new account, the boss's profile photo, slightly odd wording.</span></li>
</ol></div>

Seeing **one** sign is enough to stop. Two or more signs almost certainly mean a problem.

**Three steps to handle it:**

<div class="flow" markdown="1">
<span>1. Stop</span>
<span>2. Check through another channel</span>
<span>3. Tell the right person</span>
</div>

**Step 1. Stop.** Do not click the link, open the attachment, or reply straight away. The urgency is the trap.

**Step 2. Check through another channel.** If your boss messages on a chat app, call the number you saved earlier. If "the bank" calls, call the number printed on your card. Never use a number or link from the suspicious message itself. Some countries have an official site for checking websites and reading scam warnings. In Vietnam, for example, there is [tinnhiemmang.vn](https://tinnhiemmang.vn/), run by the National Cyber Security Center (checked 14/11/2026).

**Step 3. Tell the right person.** At work, tell your manager or the IT team, so colleagues are warned in time. If you have already sent a password, report it now and change it. If you have already sent money, call your bank at once, then report it to the police.

Reporting a mistaken click quickly is nothing to be ashamed of. Reporting early limits the damage.

## 4. Workplace example

> **Hypothetical scenario.** Alex is a new employee in the accounts team of a furniture workshop. He gets a chat message from an account with the director's photo.

> "Alex, urgently transfer 45,000 to a partner using this account number. I'm in a meeting and can't take calls. Get it done before 11."

Alex spots 3 signs: urgency, a request to transfer money, and blocking a confirmation call. He does this:

1. He does not transfer and does not reply.
2. He calls the director's number from the company contact list. The director says he sent no message.
3. He tells the chief accountant, and posts a screenshot in the internal group to warn others.

## 5. Practice

> **Hypothetical scenario.** You are an intern at a travel company. You get an email: "Your mailbox is almost full. Click here within 24 hours to keep your emails." The sender is "IT Suport", with an unfamiliar address that is not the company's domain.

Point out the warning signs, then write what you do in 3 steps.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Look at the deadline, the sender's address, and what they want you to do.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Model answer</summary>

Signs: urgency (24 hours); the address is not the company's domain; it asks you to click a link.

1. Stop: do not click the link.
2. Check: ask IT on the internal phone number, or in person.
3. Report: forward the email to IT as the company instructs, then delete it.
</details>
