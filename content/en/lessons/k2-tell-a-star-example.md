## 3. Explanation

Interviewers often ask: "Tell me about a time when you…". For example, a difficult group project, or a time you fixed a mistake. They want a real story, not self-praise such as "I'm very hard-working".

**STAR** is a way to tell it in 4 parts:

<div class="flow" markdown="1">
<span>S – Situation</span>
<span>T – Task</span>
<span>A – Action</span>
<span>R – Result</span>
</div>

| Part | Content | Suggested length |
|---|---|---|
| **S** – Situation | Where, when, what the context was | 1–2 sentences |
| **T** – Task | What you were responsible for | 1 sentence |
| **A** – Action | What you did, step by step | 3–4 sentences, the longest part |
| **R** – Result | What happened, what you learned | 1–2 sentences, with numbers |

**Three tips:**

1. **Say "I", not "we"** in the action part. The interviewer wants to know what you did.
2. **Make the result specific.** "20 more customers a week" beats "good results". With no numbers, give evidence: praise from a manager, being given more responsibility.
3. **Real stories, told truthfully.** Do not exaggerate. Interviewers often ask for detail, and invented stories show.

**Prepare in advance.** Choose 3–4 examples from study, part-time work, clubs or volunteering. Write each one out in STAR, then practise saying it aloud. The evidence journal on this site is a good source of examples.

STAR examples also help you write a CV. Each experience line should have an action and a result.

## 4. Workplace example

> **Hypothetical scenario.** Adam, a final-year student, is interviewing for a sales internship. The question: "Tell me about a time you solved a problem at work."

Adam's answer:

> **S:** Last year I worked part-time at a bubble tea shop near campus. At the lunchtime peak, customers waited about 15 minutes, and many left.
>
> **T:** The manager asked me to find a way to cut the waiting time.
>
> **A:** I watched three lunchtimes and took notes. I saw that customers spent a long time choosing. I suggested a short menu of the 8 best-sellers, placed by the door. I also suggested taking orders before customers reached the counter.
>
> **R:** After two weeks, the wait was down to about 7 minutes. The manager kept this approach for the whole shop. I learned how important it is to observe carefully before suggesting changes.

**Why this works:** the action part is longest and uses "I". The result has a number and evidence.

## 5. Practice

Choose something real you have done, at school, at a part-time job or in a club. Write a STAR answer to the question: "Tell me about a time you worked well in a team."

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Nothing comes to mind? Think of school group projects, club events, or a busy part-time shift.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Check part A: does it have at least 3 actions of your own? Part R: does it have a number or evidence?
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Model answer</summary>

**S:** Last term, my group of 4 did a survey project for our statistics course, with a 3-week deadline.

**T:** The group asked me to plan the work and track progress.

**A:** At the first meeting, I suggested a task board with owners and deadlines. I set a check-in in the middle of week two. At that point, the survey was only half done. I reorganised the work so two people did the survey together.

**R:** We submitted two days early and got a high mark. Since then I always set a check-in partway through.

(This is a hypothetical example. In an interview, use only your own real stories.)
</details>
