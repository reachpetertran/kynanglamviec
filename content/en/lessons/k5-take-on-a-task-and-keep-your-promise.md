## 1. Objectives

- Take on a task and **repeat it back**: what to do, and when it is due.
- **Say early** when you might be late, and what you need.

## 3. Explanation

People at work always **take on tasks and agree when they will be done**. A tailor agrees a day to return the clothes. A mechanic agrees a time to collect the bike. Keeping these promises helps others trust you and feel confident working with you.

**Part 1. Taking on a task properly.** Many missed deadlines start with not hearing clearly. Use 3 steps:

<div class="diagram"><p class="dg-title">Taking on a task in 3 steps</p><ol class="steps">
<li><b>Listen fully</b><span>Don't interrupt the person giving the task.</span></li>
<li><b>Repeat back</b><span>Say again: what to do, and when it is due.</span></li>
<li><b>Write the deadline</b><span>Write it on a calendar or in a notebook straight away.</span></li>
</ol></div>

**Part 2. Keeping your promise.** Don't wait until just before the deadline. Use 4 steps:

1. **Get everything you need** before you start.
2. **Check halfway.** Choose a day in the middle to ask yourself: how much have I done?
3. **If you are behind, say so early.** Keep it short: which part may be late, and what you need.
4. **Hand it in on time**, or by the new deadline you agreed.

**Saying it early is different from saying sorry late.** Saying it early means saying it while there is still time. Then others can still help you.

**Asking for help at the right time** is also a skill. Try first. If it still does not work, say clearly where you are stuck.

## 4. Workplace example

> **Hypothetical scenario.** Maya (age 11) takes on the poster decorations for her group, for the class Job fair. The deadline is Friday.

Maya repeats it back to the teacher: "OK, I'll decorate the poster and hand it in on Friday." At home, she writes it on her calendar. She chooses Wednesday as her halfway check day.

<figure class="fig"><img src="../../../assets/img/en/n1/l5-calendar.svg" alt="A weekly calendar: Monday, task given; Wednesday, halfway check; Friday, hand in, with an exclamation mark. A clock below. The words: write the deadline on your calendar, and set a halfway check day." width="640" height="400" loading="lazy"><figcaption><b>Figure 2.</b> Maya's weekly calendar: task day, halfway check day, hand-in day.</figcaption></figure>

On Wednesday, Maya finds she has run out of coloured paper. The shop is far away. She tells her teacher that same day:

<figure class="fig"><img src="../../../assets/img/en/n1/l5-say-it-early.svg" alt="The pupil says to the teacher: my part might be one day late, I need more coloured paper. The adult replies: thank you for telling me early, I have coloured paper, you can take some." width="640" height="400" loading="lazy"><figcaption><b>Figure 3.</b> Say it early, and say clearly which part is late and what you need.</figcaption></figure>

The teacher has coloured paper in the classroom. Maya finishes and hands it in on Friday.

**Why this works:** Maya repeats it back to be sure she understood. Her halfway check day helps her spot the problem early. Her message is short, and says clearly what she needs.

## 5. Practice

Say these out loud to an adult. The adult plays the person giving the task.

1. **Repeat back:** Mum says: "Water the plants before school. Remember the pot on the balcony too." How do you repeat it back?
2. **Say it early:** You agreed to make 3 Job cards for your group, due Saturday. On Thursday you have made only 1. What do you tell your group?
3. **Plan it:** Choose a task with a deadline this week. Write the task day, the halfway check day and the hand-in day.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Repeating back has 2 parts: **what** and **when**. Saying it early has 2 parts: **which part is late** and **what you need**.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Model answer</summary>

1. "OK, I water the plants before school, and the pot on the balcony too."
2. "I've only made 1 card. On Saturday I might be 1 card short. Could someone make 1 card for me?"
3. For example: task given Monday, halfway check Wednesday, hand in Friday.
</details>

## 7. Reflection

The adult asks, and you answer out loud. The site saves nothing.

1. The last time you were late, when did you first notice you were behind? What if you had said so earlier?
2. Which task do you forget most often? Where will you write it down?

## 8. Real-world application

Over the next 7 days, take on **one household task with a deadline**. Repeat it back when you take it on. Write it on your calendar. On your halfway day, check yourself and tell an adult.
