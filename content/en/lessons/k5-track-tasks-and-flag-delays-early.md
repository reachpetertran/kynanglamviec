## 3. Explanation

Keeping work commitments is a skill you can practise. It has two parts: **not forgetting tasks** and **not staying silent when you might be late**.

**Part 1. Write every task in one place.** Memory is unreliable when you have many tasks. Write each task down as soon as you get it. Choose one place only: a notebook, a notes app or a spreadsheet. Each task is one line with 5 columns:

| Task | Result | Deadline | Given by | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| October expenses summary | Spreadsheet using the template | 16:00 Friday | Helen | In progress |

Status needs only four values: *Not started, In progress, At risk of delay, Done*.

Check your list **twice a day**: at the start, to choose what to do first; at the end, to update the status.

**Part 2. Flag it early when a delay looks likely.** The earlier you tell people, the more options they have. They can move the deadline, add people or reduce the scope. At the last minute, there are almost no options left.

A good early-warning message has four parts:

1. **Status:** where the task is, how much is done.
2. **Reason:** one sentence of facts, with no blame.
3. **Options:** how you suggest handling it, perhaps two choices.
4. **Request:** what decision or help you need, and by when.

Avoid messages that only apologise. "Sorry, I probably won't make it" leaves the other person asking about everything.

When is "early"? As soon as you see your current plan is not enough to finish on time. Do not wait until the deadline day.

## 4. Workplace example

> **Hypothetical scenario.** Noah is a new office administrator. On Monday, Helen asks him to summarise October expenses by 16:00 Friday. By Wednesday, he still has no figures from the warehouse team.

Noah sets the status to "At risk of delay" and messages Helen on Wednesday:

> "Hi Helen, an update on the October expenses table. **Status:** 4 of 5 departments are done. **Reason:** the warehouse hasn't sent its figures; I reminded them on Tuesday. **Options:** (1) I send the 4-department version on time and add the warehouse later. Or (2) you remind the warehouse so I can finish everything by Friday. **Request:** could you choose an option by tomorrow lunchtime?"

**Why this works:** Helen knows on Wednesday, with two days left to act. The message has specific figures. Noah offers options, so Helen only needs to choose.

## 5. Practice

> **Hypothetical scenario.** You work at a convenience store. Your manager asks you to count the drinks shelf, done by 17:00. At 14:00, the barcode scanner breaks. Counting by hand, you estimate you will finish at 18:30.

(1) Record this task as one 5-column line and (2) write an early-warning message to your manager.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

What is the status at 14:00? How much of the shelf have you counted?
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

What options are there? For example: count by hand and move the deadline; borrow another scanner; count the best-sellers first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Model answer</summary>

Task line: *Count the drinks shelf · Complete stock count sheet · 17:00 today · Ken (manager) · At risk of delay.*

Message: "Hi Ken, an update on the drinks shelf count. **Status:** about a third of the shelf is done. **Reason:** the scanner broke at 14:00. **Options:** (1) I count by hand and finish around 18:30; or (2) I borrow the till scanner when it's quiet and may finish by 17:00. **Request:** could you choose by 14:30?"

(Names and figures are hypothetical.)

In the next 7 days:

- Create a 5-column task list in one place. Check it at the start and end of each day.
