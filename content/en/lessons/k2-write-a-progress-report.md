## 1. Objectives

- Write a progress report in 3 parts: **done, blocked, help needed**.
- Include specific **numbers** and **dates** in the report.

## 3. Explanation

Your manager needs to know where the work stands, so they can decide in time. A good progress report answers three questions, in order.

<div class="flow" markdown="1">
<span>Done</span>
<span>Blocked</span>
<span>Help needed</span>
</div>

**1. What is done?** State results, not the process. "Called 40 of 60 customers" is clearer than "worked with customers all week".

**2. What is blocked?** State the problem and how it affects the deadline. If nothing is blocked, write "None". Do not leave it blank, or the reader will not be sure.

**3. What help is needed?** Say who needs to do what, by when. If none, write "No help needed".

Add a line for **next steps** if your manager wants to know the plan.

**Three common mistakes:**

- Reporting only good news. Bad news reported early is what helps your manager adjust.
- Vague words: "nearly done", "in progress". Use numbers: 80%, 3 of 5 items.
- Reports that are too long. Readers are busy, so keep it to 5–7 lines.

**When to report?** On the agreed schedule, for example Friday afternoon. Outside the schedule, report as soon as there is a risk of delay, as in the lesson on [flagging delays early](../k5-track-tasks-and-flag-delays-early/).

**Adjust for the reader.** For your direct team leader, you can give more detail. For more senior people, keep only the main results and the decisions you need from them.

## 4. Workplace example

> **Hypothetical scenario.** Ethan is a new salesperson at an office equipment distributor. Every Friday afternoon, he sends a weekly report to his team leader by chat.

Ethan's report:

> **Week 46 report – Ethan**
>
> **Done:** called 40 of 60 existing customers; 6 meetings booked; closed 2 orders worth 7,500 in total.
>
> **Blocked:** 20 remaining customers have wrong phone numbers. I may miss the goal of calling the whole list by 30/11.
>
> **Help needed:** could you ask accounts for an updated list before Tuesday?
>
> **Next week:** meet the 6 booked customers.

**Why this works:** Each part has numbers. The problem says how it affects the deadline. The request says who does what, by when.

## 5. Practice

> **Hypothetical scenario.** You work in a warehouse. You have been asked to reorganise the dry goods area, with 5 shelving rows, by the end of the month. You have finished 3 rows. Row 4 has no shelf labels; they were ordered but the supplier has not delivered.

Write a 3-part progress report of no more than 6 lines.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Done: 3 of 5 rows. Blocked: no shelf labels for row 4. How does this affect the deadline?
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Help needed: who could chase the supplier? Or could temporary labels be used?
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Model answer</summary>

**Done:** reorganised 3 of 5 shelving rows in the dry goods area.

**Blocked:** row 4 has no shelf labels; the supplier has not delivered. If they are not here by the 25th, the end-of-month deadline may slip.

**Help needed:** could you ask purchasing to chase them before the 22nd, or approve temporary printed labels?

**Next week:** do row 5 while waiting for the labels.

(Figures and dates are hypothetical.)
</details>
